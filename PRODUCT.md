# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Venue managers who own or operate a recurring event space — a park, fairground, plaza, or flex venue. They plan many events per year across the same footprint: setting up vendor layouts, drawing borders, documenting ADA paths, calculating capacity, and exporting a PDF site plan for permits or client approval. They work fast under deadline, often on a laptop at the venue or in an office, and need the tool to get out of the way.

## Product Purpose

SITEMAP is a browser-based event site planner built on a live Google Maps satellite layer. Venue managers place elements (tents, stages, food trucks, generators, ingress/egress gates), draw zone polygons and borders, calculate capacity, check safety compliance, and export a print-ready PDF site plan — all in a single session without installing software.

Success: a venue manager opens SITEMAP, searches their venue, lays out a full event in under 20 minutes, and exports a compliant PDF ready to attach to a permit application.

## Brand Personality

Fast · Clear · Confident

The tool moves at the speed of thought. Controls are immediate and obvious. The interface earns trust through precision — measurements are real, warnings are actionable, outputs look professional. No friction, no decoration for its own sake.

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS (Notion / Linear clone)**: no cream or off-white backgrounds, no sidebar-dominant wireframe aesthetic, no "startup productivity tool" minimalism. SITEMAP is a spatial, visual tool, not a document editor.
- **Consumer mapping apps (Google Maps UI)**: not a navigation app wrapper. The map is infrastructure, not the product. SITEMAP should feel like a professional operations tool that happens to use satellite imagery.

## Design Principles

1. **The map is the canvas, not the UI.** Controls and panels exist to serve the map, not the other way around. Nothing should compete with the spatial work area for visual weight.
2. **Confidence through density.** Venue managers read dense information panels fast. Don't pad or simplify to the point of hiding useful data. Show real numbers, real codes, real warnings.
3. **Warnings earn their weight.** Safety violations are blocking work issues. Hard violations must stop the user; soft warnings should advise. Never bury compliance feedback.
4. **Export is a first-class deliverable.** The PDF is a permit document. It must look professional enough to hand to a city official without apology.
5. **Speed over ceremony.** Every added click is a broken promise. If a tool can be reached in one action, it must be.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA minimum. The tool is used under deadline pressure; focus management and keyboard shortcuts reduce friction for power users. Color is never the sole indicator of a warning state — icon + color + text always together.
